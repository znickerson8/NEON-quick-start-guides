"The Rapid Habitat Assessment protocol is performed annually at each NEON wadeable stream site during baseflow conditions, typically requiring 6-8 hours of work for two trained Field Ecologists. It consists of two components: the Habitat Survey, in which technicians work from the downstream to the upstream reach boundary delineating and characterizing each stream habitat unit (habitat type, length, maximum wetted width, maximum depth, dominant substrate, riparian cover, and large woody debris), and the Pebble Count Survey, in which substrate particle size is measured with a gravelometer at 40 points along each of five cross-sections between the left and right bankfull margins. Habitat boundaries are recorded with a GPS receiver, and data are entered in the field using a mobile application, with paper datasheets available as a backup."
